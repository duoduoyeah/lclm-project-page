Line-Coupled Language Model

*Equal contribution.
University of California, Riverside
Preprint · submitted to ACL Rolling Review

TL;DR: LCLM advances several text lines together in one forward pass, producing 2.94 content tokens per forward at only a 0.015 BPB gap to a matched autoregressive baseline.

LCLM keeps standard causal attention while advancing multiple lines in parallel — every prediction is retained as output, unlike MTP's rejected proposals or block diffusion's partial unmasking.

Why lines

Autoregressive language models generate one token per forward pass, capping how much useful output each step produces. Diffusion, insertion-based decoding, and multi-token prediction relax this, but either add training-time token traffic or struggle when future tokens strongly depend on each other.

Method

LCLM treats each text line as its own generation thread: every active line predicts its next token in the same forward pass, interleaved into a single causal sequence via line-staggered rotary positions. Training and decoding stay standard next-token prediction with causal attention — no auxiliary heads, no verification step.

Result

At 881M parameters, LCLM reaches 2.94 content tokens per forward pass at a 0.015 BPB gap to a matched autoregressive baseline (0.728 vs. 0.713 BPB at 15.5B training tokens), and the gap stays small (≤0.028 BPB) even as the active-line count climbs past 16. Full ablations, CORE benchmark results, and limitations are in the paper.
